Steam Player Pulse: bots, sales and spikes in 1.9M player counts

Satwik Kotta · Steam concurrent players and store prices, August to November 2026 · Dashboard · Code

Questions

Player counts decide which games Steam shows and how publishers judge a launch or a sale. Three questions a platform, trust and safety or live operations team would ask of them: whose players are real people, what a discount does to players, and what is behind sudden spikes.

Findings

  • A flat day is a fingerprint of play without a person. Real players sleep, so the typical game has 2.2x more players at its busiest hour than its quietest (Counter-Strike 2 2.4x, PUBG 5.8x). Bongo Cat (1.36x), Banana, known for item farming (1.37x), and MIR4 (1.13x) barely move. Ranking 1,807 established games by flatness, 48% of the 25 flattest are idle games by name, against 2.7% overall, an 18x lift (AUC 0.85). This is a cheap first screen for inflated player counts, not proof of bots.
  • The Autumn Sale lifted players 28% (95% CI 25 to 32%), and most of the lift stayed. 1,559 discounted games against 250 paid games at full price, with game and day fixed effects. Two weeks after the sale ended the gap was still 17% (12 to 22%). Trends before the sale were flat (mean absolute 1.5% a day). Deeper discounts lifted more: 25% for up to 33% off, 31% for 67% or more.
  • A discount on its own did about twice as much. Across 3,111 discounts outside the big sale (median 65% off), players rose 56% (52 to 60%) in the first week, peaking near 78% on day 6. During the sale, 81% of tracked games were discounted at once, so each one competed for the same attention.
  • Most spikes are not about price. Of 275 player spikes in paid games (players at least doubled against the last two weeks, beyond five times normal noise), 55% came with no price change: updates, new seasons, free weekends and streamers. A price feed alone misses most of what moves a game.

Data and checks

  • Two public git scraped collections read at pinned commits: 1,922,759 player count samples for 35,244 games from Steam's public API, sampled about daily at a rotating hour, and store prices in 8 regions twice a day. Loaded into DuckDB.
  • Every UTC hour is covered, which is what makes the daily cycle measurable. 5 samples where the API declined to answer are kept as missing, not zero. No duplicate samples. 79 of 4.39M US price rows have a discount that disagrees with the prices.
  • The price watchlist grew from 6,386 to 31,550 games over the period, so games added late are left out of comparisons that need two weeks of prices before the sale.

Limits

Three months of data. Games choose whether to join a sale, so the comparison controls for each game's level and each calendar day, not for why a game was discounted. Lift is in concurrent players, not revenue. Idle names are a weak label, and an idle game is not a cheat: the cycle flags play without a person, which a team would then investigate.

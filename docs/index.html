Steam Player Pulse

What 1.9 million player count samples across Steam say about bots and idle play, sales, and sudden player spikes. Data: concurrent players sampled daily at rotating hours for thousands of Steam games, and store prices twice a day, August to November 2026. Code and method · One page memo

1 · Who is really playing: the shape of a game's day

A day of players, by game

Players by UTC hour relative to the game's daily average, from each game's fitted daily cycle. Real players sleep. Idle games and farming do not.

Flat days flag idle and bot driven play

Each dot is a game with 200+ players on average. Up is a stronger daily swing (busiest hour over quietest). Orange: the name says idle, clicker, AFK or desktop.

The flattest days on Steam

Peak to trough ratio with a 95% bootstrap interval. 1.0 means the same number of players at every hour.

When each game is busiest

Number of games by the UTC hour of their daily peak. Evening in China is about 12 to 14 UTC, Europe 17 to 20 UTC, the Americas 0 to 3 UTC.

3 · Sudden spikes

What was behind each spike

A spike: a paid game with 50+ players at least doubles against its last two weeks, beyond five times its normal day to day noise, after removing store wide days.

Biggest spikes

Players against the game's two week baseline. A spike with no price change is usually an update, a new season, a free weekend or a streamer.
